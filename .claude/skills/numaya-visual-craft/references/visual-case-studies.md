# Visual case studies and transferable lessons

IDs and evidence labels resolve in [video-source-index.md](video-source-index.md). “Caption evidence” means an exported YouTube auto-transcript was read. “Observed” means an actual playback screenshot was inspected. Timestamp ranges are navigation aids, not pixel-accurate frame logs.

No screenshot assets are bundled. No complete before/after pair was visually inspected under identical conditions. Where the creator describes an earlier screen, that is labeled as narration rather than independent visual verification.

## 1. Alarm app: art direction is a set of decisions

**Source:** TG01, [3:49–6:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=229s), [7:06–7:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=426s), [10:05–10:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=605s).

**Interface / problem:** Tim compares four AI-created alarm-app treatments. His explanation contrasts an ordinary first draft, a more directed version, an overloaded reference mix, and a coherent concept.

**What he explains:** Better vocabulary can improve spacing and typography, while too many competing references can damage consistency. The final example is organized around waking gently. A recurring sunrise treatment, considered numeric hierarchy, and human language support that idea.

**Observed:** In a paused frame sampled around 10:14–10:22, two phone mockups appear together on a light presentation canvas. Their screens use dark purple with a bright pink/purple glow. Large central numerals dominate one screen; the other uses a smaller set of numeric/text information. This supports a shared visual vocabulary and deliberate difference in emphasis. An earlier loaded image with grade columns was a thumbnail, not verified playback evidence.

**Transferable technique:** Write a specific intent, then use it to decide what belongs and what to cut. Review point of view, consistency, and execution separately. The exact three-axis scorecard is directly explained at 0:52–1:18; the detailed Numaya rubric is our synthesis.

**Numaya application (S):** A discovery layout could emphasize a person and a conversation cue with a strong Fraunces name, a considered photo region, and subordinate Inter metadata. The principle is coherent intent, not copying gradients or alarm widgets.

**When not:** Do not turn “waking gently” into Numaya's theme, imitate the app's artwork/copy, or redesign a functional control merely to look novel.

**Limits:** No complete controlled visual comparison of all four versions was inspected. The creator's scores and claims about AI are evaluative opinions, not measured performance data.

## 2. Undertone: settle visual language before polishing individual flows

**Source:** TG02, [7:27–9:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YA3QpYIKqNI&t=447s).

**Interface / problem:** The therapy-recording app's founders describe an overly utilitarian experience. They want personality without a sterile or overly cute tone.

**What he explains:** The team defines brand direction before redesigning flows: muted greens, off-whites, charcoal, type, icons, components, and button states. A palette alone does not resolve the full look.

**Observed:** A frame sampled around 8:24–8:29 shows a palette presentation: two muted green swatches, pale neutral swatches, and a very dark charcoal swatch on a light canvas. No exact color values, font specifications, or screen spacing can be inferred.

**Technique / reasoning:** Turn emotional adjectives into a small, implementable system so different flows can feel like one product.

**Numaya application (S/B):** Keep the established white/neutral, purple/lavender, Fraunces/Inter direction. Audit how it is expressed in actual headings, chips, buttons, and image treatments before creating new ones.

**When not:** Do not rebrand Numaya in Undertone's colors or assume “premium” means clinical minimalism. An existing coherent system may need only a few corrections.

**Limits:** We observed the palette, not a full visual before/after system audit. The broader component foundation is caption evidence.

## 3. Undertone: give a meaningful moment a fitting surface

**Source:** TG02, [6:35–6:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YA3QpYIKqNI&t=395s), [14:48–16:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YA3QpYIKqNI&t=888s), 18:23–18:49.

**Interface / problem:** Tim says the old post-session payoff was a text block and a recap button. The redesigned recap is presented as something worth opening after an emotionally important session.

**What he explains:** A wrapped recap, a user-controlled reveal, and carefully chosen sensory details create a moment consistent with the therapy context. He later ties the sleeve/record metaphor to a personal mental-health record.

**Observed:** Around 15:02–15:08, a light presentation slide shows a phone with a pale screen, a centered pink sleeve-like object, and a light circular disc form. This is a static visual sample. We did not inspect the complete peel animation or assess its implementation.

**Technique / reasoning:** Make presentation correspond to what the user has actually gained. A “success” screen should reflect the value and emotional context of its particular event.

**Numaya application (S):** A mutual-match screen can give visual prominence to the two people and a real shared interest, followed by a clear conversation action. Test the still frame before any motion; the pilot deliberately omits animation.

**When not:** Do not add a gift reveal to every event, copy the record sleeve, obscure the next action, or make a celebration imply a relationship that does not exist.

**Limits:** The old screen is described in captions, not independently inspected. No causal retention uplift is established.

## 4. Premium states: consistency continues beyond the happy path

**Source:** TG03, [2:45–4:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=165s), [5:40–6:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=340s), [8:15–9:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=495s).

**Interface / problem:** Caption evidence describes an Undertone mic test that ends without useful output and separate quota screens with vague messaging.

**What he explains:** Replace inconsistent, uninformative states with a coherent pattern that communicates actual usage and refresh timing; inspect failure and empty states deliberately.

**Observable evidence status:** No relevant screen frame was inspected. Concrete count/date examples are his verbal account, not our visual observation.

**Technique / reasoning:** Users evaluate a product across connected states. A clear status, explanation, and available next action help a polished interface remain understandable.

**Numaya application (S):** If verification fails, explain the real failure and available retry path; if discovery is empty, use the same type and component grammar as populated discovery. Preserve entered profile/interests data when supported.

**When not:** Do not display fake usage counts, made-up progress, refresh dates, or unsupported recovery controls. Do not treat clear copy as a substitute for visual hierarchy.

**Limits:** His business/research claims are not independently validated. This source supports completeness and coherence much more strongly than a specific typography recipe.

## 5. Solcard: a product object can carry the brand

**Source:** TG04, [1:29–3:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0GMQRCnOQmo&t=89s).

**Interface / problem:** Tim describes a dated, rushed-looking starting point that did not communicate confidence. Brand, type, card objects, website, setup, and app are treated together.

**What he explains:** The card itself becomes a recurring thread across touchpoints rather than relying only on the logo. Its presentation bridges the initial impression and product use.

**Observed:** In a frame sampled around 2:12–2:17, a single dark payment card sits centrally against a black field, with a white brand mark and payment-network mark. The card has restrained visible tonal/material variation. The screenshot does not show all four narrated card colors or the full website/app sequence.

**Technique / reasoning:** Use an object or content form native to the product to provide continuity; repeat a meaningful visual relationship rather than unrelated decoration.

**Numaya application (S):** A person's portrait/name/shared-interest grouping can retain recognizable relationships across discovery, profile, and match. A person is not a collectible card; preserve dignity and clarity.

**When not:** Do not copy payment-card materials, black fintech styling, or add metallic gradients to Numaya. Do not infer trustworthiness from cosmetic polish alone.

**Limits:** The earlier website and cross-platform sequence were not independently visually compared. Reported business outcomes are observational creator claims, not causal proof.

## 6. Indie apps: personality belongs in core information

**Source:** TG05, [1:31–4:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MAvKHToGiVY&t=91s), [6:30–8:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MAvKHToGiVY&t=390s).

**Interface / problem:** Caption evidence contrasts personality added at the edges with personality shaping the core surface. Carrot presents weather through contextual character reactions; Day One enriches ordinary entry text with contextual detail and craft.

**Evidence status:** Described in captions; no Carrot, Forest, or Day One frames inspected.

**Technique / reasoning:** Improve the way meaningful information is delivered. An empty-state mascot cannot compensate for a forgettable core screen.

**Numaya application (S):** Present real prompts and interests as cues for conversation rather than a wall of equally styled labels. Use existing truthful context to give profiles personality.

**When not:** Do not copy Carrot's insults or add a mascot by default. Do not adopt the video's loss/stakes mechanisms for dating. Do not expose private metadata to create apparent richness.

**Limits:** These examples support an experience argument; they do not establish specific layout metrics or validate the described apps' current interfaces.

## 7. Onboarding: justify each piece of work

**Source:** TG06, [1:12–2:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Aa89MC8jX2c&t=72s), [2:23–4:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Aa89MC8jX2c&t=143s).

**Interface / problem:** The narration contrasts a short path to value in Granola with longer personalization flows in health apps.

**Technique / reasoning (D):** Extra onboarding can be justified by useful personalization; brevity is not a universal rule. The video also discusses filtering buyers, which this skill does not adopt.

**Numaya application (S):** Make the benefit of profile/interests steps clear, group the fields, and preserve genuinely necessary matching inputs. Critique the visual density independently of deciding to delete steps.

**When not:** Do not remove required eligibility, consent, safety, or matching information for a cleaner screenshot. Do not copy the exact two-screen flow into dating.

**Limits:** Caption-only evidence; no screen counts or company-growth claims independently verified. This supports task relevance, not a visual style.

## 8. Audience-specific representation and category assumptions

**Sources:** TG07 [1:05–4:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q&t=65s), [7:21–9:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q&t=441s); TG08 [1:00–1:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=60s).

**Interface / problem:** TG07 describes a visual planner replacing conventional text rows with duration-based illustrated blocks, and a tracker avoiding shame. TG08 describes Timeleft challenging the assumption that people must browse profiles before meeting.

**Technique / reasoning (D):** Start from the actual audience and emotional context rather than assuming the category leader's structure is mandatory.

**Numaya application (S):** Ask whether interests and prompts are presented in a way that supports conversation, and whether inactivity language feels judgmental. Keep the pilot's existing product flows intact; treat new interaction models as separate proposals.

**When not:** Do not infer that Numaya should remove discovery, replace profiles with dinners, or adopt visually rich blocks everywhere. Audience-specific examples are not universal findings.

**Limits:** No relevant frames inspected. Auto-caption names may be inaccurate. TG07's title mentions six apps but its narration groups four patterns; preserve that discrepancy rather than inventing missing examples.
